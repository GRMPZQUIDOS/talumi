# Certification and the registry

## Open engine, certified service

The Talumi engine is free software (GNU AGPL v3). Anyone can run it.

The Talumi certificate is issued by the Talumi service only. It states that a translator listed in the Talumi registry examined the critical passages of a precise document, with the Talumi method, and signed it.

## The registry

To be listed, a translator provides proof of identity, professional details and qualifications. A remote assessment covers translation skills and the Talumi checking method. The listing states the language pairs and fields recognised, and their limits. Skill with the tool is not general competence in every language pair.

## The certificate

Each certificate is attached to one exact document, its date and the scope of validation. It carries a code. The client enters the code on the Talumi website and sees the translator, the document fingerprint and the date. A certificate that cannot be verified there is not a Talumi certificate.

A Talumi certificate is not, by itself, a sworn translation. It documents a professional check and a named person who answers for it.

## How a translator works with Talumi

1. The translator credits their Talumi account.
2. They submit the document. Talumi shows the price of the check.
3. They set the price they will charge their own client.
4. They receive:
   - the translation, documented and highlighted by zones at risk and zones to validate, for their own review;
   - the clean text, ready to deliver;
   - the client report, showing only the translator's own price.

The translator sells the service under their own name, at their own price.
