# TALUMI · तलुमि · タルミ · 塔鲁米
## White Paper — Professional Translation and Meaning Verification by Independent Paths

Founding Enhanced Version — October 17, 2026
## Overview

Talumi is a professional translation system driven by a human translator. It combines a translation for which the professional assumes responsibility for the choices made with a set of research and checks conducted by artificial intelligence models, following separate and initially blind paths. These checks serve to highlight difficulties, document points of convergence, and identify passages whose consequences require human validation.

The reference example is unique: translating the Italian Civil Code into French. The client orders a professional French translation. Passages through Swedish, Korean, or other expressly different languages are internal tools for investigating meaning. They do not constitute a service for delivering the code in multiple languages.

The work begins with two separate branches. The translator produces her translation from the original Italian. Simultaneously, models examine this original and produce analyses or intermediate translations without knowing the results of the other branches or the professional translation. Once their results are recorded, Talumi confronts them. It presents to the professional the divergences, recurring difficulties, and textual knots: places where an error, even unlikely, would have significant consequences.

The translator exercises dual expertise: experience with languages, usage, and real life; understanding of the functioning and limits of the machine process. She arbitrates problems and validates important passages. Her decisions feed a contextualized memory of translation difficulties.

Talumi’s ambition is to achieve reliability superior to that of a human translation performed without this system and to that of automated translation alone. This superiority constitutes the measurable objective of the product. It must be established by independent comparisons, focusing primarily on errors that change meaning and their consequences.

The professional positioning can be summarized as follows: **Talumi is the tool for signed translations.** An identifiable, qualified, and reachable professional examines critical points, validates a precise version, and signs it. The targeted community is primarily translators, with a planned European anchoring. The sought launch is a functional beta website, accompanied by useful integrations, allowing immediate trial of the method and understanding of the service that the translator can offer.
## 1. The Problem Talumi Addresses

A translation can be fluent yet inaccurate. In legal texts, a change in condition, exception, or subject can alter the scope of an article without producing an obviously incorrect sentence. Therefore, proofreading must examine the relationships expressed by the text, beyond the quality of the French language.

Human expertise allows for understanding these relationships, but attention and available time remain limited. Exhaustive proofreading alone does not guarantee that every difficulty will be recognized. Conversely, an automated system can multiply checks while confidently repeating an erroneous interpretation.

Talumi organizes their complementarity: extensive automated investigation, verifiable elements, and professional judgment. It aims to make the review process more efficient by distinguishing what is doubtful, what is important, and what is already documented.
## 2. A Unique Translation and Multiple Control Paths

The working file contains a reference original in Italian and a professional French translation. The version of the original, its date, origin, and scope are established before processing. For a code, the articles, paragraphs, titles, and cross-references retain stable identifiers.

The linguistic paths may notably include:

- Italian → direct semantic analysis;
- Italian → French, for an internal counter-version comparison;
- Italian → Swedish → French;
- Italian → Korean → French;
- other languages chosen according to the distinctions they may reveal and the capabilities of the available models.

Seven languages constitute an initial working hypothesis, not a guarantee of quality nor a number imposed on all files. Their selection must be tested. A language distant from Italian may reveal a difficulty, but it may also introduce additional loss. Linguistic distance is a means of exploration, not a certificate of reliability.

Intermediate versions remain control documents. The version delivered to the client is the one the translator has developed, corrected, and validated. No vote among model outputs automatically replaces it.
## 3. The Methodological Principle

### 3.1 Independent Blind Paths

The chosen term is “control by independent blind paths.” Each branch works without access to the conclusions of the others. It receives the necessary source text and the instructions provided for its task, then submits a fixed result before the comparison.

In an Italian → Korean → French path, the second step necessarily receives the Korean output from the first. The blindness applies to the other paths and to the professional translation: it does not eliminate internal dependencies within the path.

The orchestration system controls inputs, access, and the order of operations. Independence is organized by the software; it does not rely on a promise made by a model. The initial human branch is also recorded before the translator consults the alternative versions, in order to preserve a professional reading that has not been influenced by them.

### 3.2 A Contradictory Review After the Analyses

The comparison then takes place. The term “contradictory” here refers to the testing of an interpretation by other readings and by the original text. It is a control method, without any assimilation to a judicial procedure.

The comparison seeks disagreements, but also fragile agreements: several outputs may reproduce the same error. Distinct models may share training data or interpretation habits. Truly separate calls therefore do not guarantee statistically independent errors.

Any resolution returns to the Italian passage, its context, and, when necessary, documented references. Consensus between versions is an indicator; justification of the decision remains necessary.
## 4. Successive Research and Checks

### Research 1 — Define the Purpose and Use of the Text

The professional specifies the recipient, the expected use, the field, the jurisdiction, and the possible consequences of an error. For the example of the Italian Civil Code, translating Italian law into French does not mean adapting that law to French law. A seemingly familiar French term may evoke a different institution: this proximity must be examined.

### Research 2 — Map the Structure and Data

Talumi identifies articles, definitions, lists, conditions, exceptions, deadlines, amounts, and internal references. It verifies the integrity of the extraction and the alignment between source and translation. A missing passage or a misplaced reference must remain visible independently of any linguistic assessment.

### Research 3 — Identify Sensitive Notions

Separate analyses search for polysemous terms, concepts specific to a legal system, historical usages, fixed expressions, and context dependencies. Each difficulty is located and explained. The system distinguishes a known difficulty from a new issue to be addressed.

### Research 4 — Explore Linguistic Paths

Branches produce their results without consulting each other. Languages are chosen to diversify readings and test certain distinctions. The actual capability of the model in each language is taken into account: a less well-mastered language may produce more noise than useful information.

### Research 5 — Examine Intermediate Steps

A final French translation that appears correct may mask a loss in an intermediate step. Talumi therefore retains outputs at each step and organizes their review by distinct evaluators, with recourse to human linguistic expertise when the problem cannot be resolved otherwise. An automatic quality score remains an estimate.

### Research 6 — Compare Semantic Relations

The comparison focuses notably on the persons concerned, actions, obligations, permissions, prohibitions, conditions, exceptions, and effects over time. It distinguishes differences in formulation from differences in scope. The relevant passages are presented with precise excerpts, without producing a mere average of the versions.

### Research 7 — Search for Useful References

Disputed notions may require consultation of definitions, institutional sources, glossaries, or validated precedents. Each reference retains its origin, date, and field of application. A source suggestion by a model is usable only after verification of its existence and relevant content.

### Research 8 — Determine Nodes and Their Importance

An analysis of the source text identifies passages that require human validation due to their consequences, even without detected disagreement. This analysis is distinct from error detection: a passage may be stable across all branches and remain critical.

### Research 9 — Submit Points for Human Decision

The translator receives an organized file: source passage, professional translation, concordant or divergent elements, references, stakes, and question to resolve. She may maintain her translation, modify it, request an investigation, or consult a specialist. Each decision is justified and recorded.

### Research 10 — Control the Final Version

Corrections are followed by a consistency check throughout the document. A terminological choice may affect several articles; a local correction may modify a reference or a definition. Talumi also retains points left open, without turning them into implicit validations.
## 5. The Same Issue Reported Through Multiple Paths

**If the same question appears in the review of the Italian text and in the path passing through Korean, both reports are retained and explicitly notified.**

The interface groups them into a common folder while maintaining their origin:

| Element | Example Presentation |
|---|---|
| Common Question | Does the condition apply to the entire obligation or only to one of its components? |
| Report 1 | Analysis of the Italian text: scope of the condition to be verified |
| Report 2 | Italian → Korean → French path: different interpretation of this scope |
| Status | Question reported twice; human decision awaited |

This example describes a hypothetical situation and does not cite any actual article of the code. The two occurrences increase the visibility of the question. They are not presented as two independent proofs of an error. The system also indicates whether the branches use the same model or a common step.

An isolated report remains accessible. It may be the only one to have revealed a real difficulty; the majority should not erase it.
## 6. Text Nodes and the Scale of Consequences

A node is a passage where the meaning, structure, or consequences of the text justify particular attention. Two dimensions remain separate: detected uncertainty and the possible severity of an error.

| Possible Consequence | Little Detected Uncertainty | Significant Uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Sampling control according to protocol | Targeted verification |
| Significant | Enhanced control according to usage | Priority human verification |
| Critical | Systematic human validation | In-depth validation, with additional expertise if necessary |

This grid constitutes a starting point to be calibrated. Priority takes into account the consequences, the extent of effects, and the difficulty of discovering the error after delivery. It is not reduced to a model confidence score.

### In the Italian Civil Code

Controls focus particularly on provisions that grant or withdraw rights, impose an obligation, organize liability, set a condition or deadline, or introduce an exception. Their importance depends on their effect and their links with the rest of the code. A short definition or an apparent detail can govern numerous articles and become critical.

### In a Plan Intended to Build a Tower

The same principle would lead to highlighting dimensions, units, loads, tolerances, signs, numerical values, and technical references. These data would receive double control: fidelity between source and translation, then verification by the competent professional according to usage. Verifying the translation of a value does not certify the structural calculation that produced it.

### In Ordinary Text

“The little house had a red roof and the sky was blue” generally receives low priority. In a testimony where the color identifies a building, it can become important. Classification therefore depends as much on the purpose of the text as on the words used.

### The Double Check — Verifying What Absolutely Must Be Correct

**Talumi signals what seems false and ensures verification of what absolutely must be correct.** Two reasons therefore trigger a check: uncertainty about fidelity and the importance of the passage for the document’s use. An apparently faithful passage may require systematic human confirmation. The double check is a validation procedure, even when it leads to no correction.

The mission protocol determines the categories to be checked: obligations, exceptions, liabilities, deadlines, amounts, units, or safety instructions. AI proposes their identification based on the source text and its context; the translator can add overlooked points or review their importance. The quality of identifying critical points must itself be evaluated.

Each point presents together the original, the translation, the context, and an explicit question, for example: “Is the liability ceiling identical in both versions, with the same conditions and exceptions?” Proposed actions are to validate, correct, or request clarification. A correction triggers re-examination of the concerned passage.

#### Control States in the Interface

- **Orange — doubt detected**: a possible difference in meaning must be examined.
- **Blue — mandatory double check**: the passage is important, even without suspected error.
- **Green — validated**: a decision has been recorded for this version.

Colors are always accompanied by accessible labels. The reason, priority, and status remain distinct information: a validated point retains the reason for its initial control. The protocol can prevent closing the mission as long as a mandatory point remains open.

#### Individual, Repeated, or Collective Validation

Three modes are distinguished in the interface and in the report:

1. **Individual review**: the translator examines a passage and answers the control question.
2. **Application of a decision to identical occurrences**: the translator sees the list of occurrences and confirms the scope of reuse. Identical words in different contexts do not automatically constitute the same case.
3. **Collective acceptance under the translator’s responsibility**: the translator sees the number and nature of selected points and accepts them without individual examination of each, when the mission protocol allows it. This action is not subsequently described as a series of individual double checks.

Repeating correct controls does not prove that subsequent ones will be correct. The most critical points can therefore be excluded from collective validation by the protocol. Professional decision freedom is accompanied by an exact description of the work performed.

Each decision retains the identity of the verifier, the date, the version of the passage, the validation mode, and, as applicable, the justification or rule applied. Any subsequent modification affecting the passage or its relevant context reopens the concerned controls. The absence of error after verification remains a useful result: it establishes that an important element has been examined, without guaranteeing the infallibility of the entire document.
## 7. The Translator as the Operator of the System

The professional defines the task, produces the translation, reviews the control points, and makes the final decisions. Their experience allows them to recognize usage, implicit meanings, and concrete consequences. Their understanding of the machine process enables them to interpret alerts, request additional checks, and distinguish between proof of execution and proof of accuracy.

Talumi must save them investigation time while improving risk coverage. It must not shift all their work toward handling false alerts. The presentation of cases, their accuracy, and their priority order are therefore part of the product’s quality.

Targeted supervision does not exclude a full review when the mission’s level of requirement demands it. Sampling checks of areas without alerts remain necessary to estimate what the system lets pass.
## 8. A Progressive Memory of Difficulties

Each validated case can enrich a structured database: source passage, concept, languages, domain, jurisdiction, context, difficulty, considered solutions, final choice, justification, references, and validation author.

The initial categories include polysemy, concepts without exact equivalents, historical usages, differences between legal systems, negations, conditions and exceptions, numerical data, omissions, and inconsistencies between passages.

A solution remains tied to its conditions of use. It does not become a universal substitution. Decisions are versioned and can be revised. Reuse between clients respects the confidentiality rules of the file.

The method progresses through human feedback: significant undetected difficulty, excessive alert, poorly estimated priority, or insufficient reference. Each evolution is evaluated on cases distinct from those used to design it. The accumulation of decisions alone is not sufficient to prove improvement.
## 9. Proving the Actual Execution of Controls

Talumi must be able to establish that the announced models have indeed been called. The orchestration software executes the requests; a model cannot replace this execution with a narrative claiming that it consulted other models.

The log of each branch records the provider, the model declared by the API, the available parameters, the start and end times, the request identifier when it exists, the inputs and outputs or their fingerprints, the dependency steps, and any failures. Sensitive data is protected according to the terms of the mission.

Network information, including IP addresses when their collection is relevant, may complement these traces. However, an IP address alone is not sufficient to identify the executed model: multiple models may share an infrastructure. The proof relies on a set of coherent traces originating from the software and the providers.

A failed branch is displayed as such. A replacement model or a retry is explicitly identified. The final file distinguishes between planned, executed, incomplete, and unperformed controls. Logs are retained with protections against untracked modifications.
## 10. A Billable Professional Service

The first product is a professional translation service accompanied by documented quality control. The client receives:

- the validated French translation;
- a summary of the scope, methodology, and controls performed;
- a report of key issues and their resolution;
- any reservations or open questions affecting usage;
- the traceability elements stipulated in the contract.

Billing takes into account volume, technical complexity, level of control, and arbitration work. Automated analyses are means of service production; the delivered value includes professional judgment and its documentation.

The operational prototype may focus on a single direction, Italian → French, with a limited number of paths actually executed and logged. Expansion to seven languages occurs according to their measured utility. The essential steps for the initial service are source preservation, initial blinding, comparison, prioritization, human validation, and traceability.
## 11. Demonstrating the Desired Superiority

Talumi aims to outperform human translation without this tool and to surpass AI alone. The evaluation compares three conditions: standard professional translation, automated translation alone, and professional translation with Talumi.

The same delivery requirements are applied. The texts include both ordinary and difficult passages, with sufficient context to understand them. Qualified evaluators review the results without knowing their source. A comparison with a comparable budget complements the quality measurement to assess economic value.

The priority indicators are residual errors that alter meaning, their severity, detection of critical issues, false alarms, and the professional time required. Areas without alerts are also examined. Results are presented by domain, language, and difficulty category; a good average does not mask the most serious errors.

The contribution of pivot languages is tested separately: do they provide useful insights beyond direct checks and separate models? The number of paths and languages are adjusted based on this response.
## 12. The Advantage Sought at the Scale of an Entire Text

Talumi makes feasible the parallel and repeated exploration of a text through multiple languages, models, and controls, followed by the organization of their results for a professional. A human team could reproduce some of these processes; systematically multiplying them at this scale would require considerable skills, time, and budget.

The expected advantage is therefore an extended investigative capacity, combined with responsible human judgment. The use of Italian, Swedish, or Korean is valuable for the problems it reveals and the decisions it helps to better justify.

Talumi must enable answering, for each case file: what has been verified, by which paths, which points required particular attention, and on what basis were they validated?
## 13. Positioning: Talumi’s Unique Combination

Talumi establishes the following combination as its signature:

- languages with different roots and logics, used as instruments to verify meaning;
- paths conducted blindly, confronted only after their results have been submitted;
- a translator who manages the process and makes every decision;
- classification of passages according to the severity of a possible error;
- proof that each announced check has actually been performed.

Talumi relies on well-known practices: combining multiple translations, comparing models, and back-translation. A round-trip provides an indicator of stability without proving fidelity. Mehrparvar and Pezzelle (2024) study the detection of ambiguity based on internal model representations and back-translations. This work constitutes a research avenue; it does not directly validate the Talumi system based on the confrontation of textual outputs via API. Talumi proposes to bring these approaches together in a professional service and to evaluate the specific contribution of linguistic diversity. Source: https://aclanthology.org/2024.mrl-1.26/
## 14. An Open Source Project for Independent Translators

Talumi is designed as an open source project aimed at translation professionals, particularly freelancers. It provides them with a common and inspectable control tool. Automated versions can be involved in investigations; the final service remains validated and signed by a professional. Open code, access to the service, and the terms of use of any potential label are distinct issues.

Credibility comes from the community. Open code is read, tested, and corrected publicly. A control method gains trust when everyone can verify how it operates.

### Sovereignty and Confidentiality

Talumi carries an international ambition and envisions an initial professional anchoring in Europe. Local operation or on European servers must be distinguished from the destination of calls to models: a local application may transmit text to external providers. Confidentiality depends on the actual data path, access, and retention, not solely on the interface’s location.

Three modes should be considered: fully local processing without external transmission; expressly selected providers with visible destinations; hosted service whose processing and retention are documented. The promise of data control must correspond to the configuration actually executed. No assumed transfer to a government is presented as fact without proof.

### Making a Living from Open Source

Hosting, installation, training, and support constitute offers to be tested around the open code. A comparison with companies like Odoo provides inspiration, not a model transferable without examination. API, infrastructure, and support costs are measured from the first assignments. The essential mechanisms for understanding the controls must remain inspectable.
## 15. The Core Need — An Invisible Fault with Real Consequences

**The danger lies in a false translation that appears correct.** A reader who is not proficient in the target language may confuse fluency with fidelity. A Swedish sequence can result in a plausible Italian text that expresses a different relationship between facts, people, or their obligations.

The fear of error acts as a driver for adoption: the client wants to be able to commit without discovering too late that they had understood something else. This concern must be linked to documented risks and observable controls. The frequency and severity of errors depend on the text, the language pair, and the system.

François refers to the observed nonsense as “artificial stupidity,” in contrast to the only brilliant performances highlighted. For the product, the operational category is that of **translation errors with consequences**: hallucinations, mistranslations, omissions, changes in negation, condition, or obligation. A hallucination here refers to content not based on the original; not all errors are hallucinations.

A model can produce a plausible sequence without indicating that it has lost a semantic relationship. It is not necessary to attribute to it an intention to conceal its misunderstanding. Talumi must enable examination of the result and its consequences, without claiming to deduce the internal mechanism of an error from its appearance alone.
## 16. Signed Translations and the Identifiable Professional

The simple limit proposed for the Talumi service is the **signed translation**. Behind each delivery is a professional who has examined the critical points and validated an identifiable version.

The presentation formula is: **“An identified translator, a signed translation, a professional who stands behind their work.”** It expresses the desire to link the tool to a real, qualified, and reachable person. The signature must be attached to the exact document, its date, and the scope of validation. It does not automatically mean that the translation is sworn.

The notion of recourse is part of the client’s need. Its precise effects will depend on contractual commitments and applicable law. The document does not claim that a text generated by AI would eliminate all rights of recourse; it notes that an automatic output alone does not necessarily identify the professional who assumes its fidelity. Any enforceability concerns documents and commitments, according to their legal framework, rather than a general qualification of the person.

### Registry and Qualification — Planned Development

A registry could verify identity, professional contact details, CV, diplomas or qualifications, and VAT number when applicable. Age has been mentioned among registration information; its necessity remains to be determined. Verification documents and private contact details remain separate from what is publicly displayed.

A remote examination could assess translation skills and mastery of Talumi control, with a procedure for verifying identity and the integrity of the assessment. The qualification would specify recognized language pairs, domains, and limits. General competence in the tool does not equate to universal competence in translation.

It is necessary to distinguish initial professional qualification, qualification for the Talumi process, and validation of a particular assignment. A diploma, test, or registration alone does not guarantee each delivery. Conditions for renewal, revision, and contestation of the qualification remain to be defined.

### Open Source and Professional Service

The production service and the use of any potential label could be reserved for a community of verified professionals. The open-source code can remain accessible to all. Restricting code usage to only graduates would conflict with the open-source objective; therefore, access rules for the service and the brand must be defined separately. A public demonstration on publishable examples can coexist with this professional access.
## 17. Transforming the Profession and Making the Service Marketable

Talumi aims to make AI an ally of the translator. The professional spends more time defining the task, examining key points, and making decisions, while the system automates part of the repetitive investigations. The transition is compared to the shift from the typewriter to the computer: the way of working changes profoundly.

The stated objective is work that is potentially ten times faster and of better quality. This factor of ten remains a target to be measured, including verifications, false alerts, research, and corrections. The reduction in production time may affect pricing; the translator must be able to maintain compensation linked to their expertise and the service actually provided.

The commercial value must be visible: final translation, consequential points examined, motivated decisions, and signature. An overall score or a green light does not replace this dossier and could erase the professional contribution. It is necessary to test whether clients accept paying for this service and if it improves the translator’s actual profitability.

The first planned economic trial is a project with a partner translator. Measurements focus on quality, total time, technical costs, and the usefulness of the report for the client. The risk to monitor is that the tool is used solely to demand lower prices without recognizing the validation work.
## 18. A European Professional Community

A European approach has been proposed: a community of translators who document the difficulties of languages, usages, and institutions within their market. Coverage of 45 languages is an ambition to be specified, not a fixed list nor a capability already demonstrated. It is not to be confused with the 24 official languages of the European Union.

Coverage of European languages by major tools already exists. Romanian, in particular, should not be presented as inaccessible to these systems. The sought-after advantage lies in the knowledge of difficulties, professional validation, and contextualized resources. The European Commission itself offers tools covering the official languages of the Union and certain additional languages: https://translation.ec.europa.eu/tools-and-resources/ai-translation-and-language-tools_en

The community can be structured by language pair and by domain. Contributors enrich difficult cases, glossaries, and protocols. Mastery of one language is not sufficient to validate all pairs in which it is involved.

Europe here defines a first market and a network of expertise. Internal control languages can remain broader: Korean can retain experimental utility. The predominance of English does not eliminate the need to verify the consistency of commitments.
## 19. Launch — a usable beta site and a comprehensible service

The direction chosen by François is to prepare a **functional beta site**, with the necessary integrations for use, and then to make it known directly to translators. Publishing a repository and waiting for a community does not constitute a launch. This white paper describes this target; it does not attest to the existence of an already operational site or plugins.

The professional must understand in one paragraph what they can sell. The work pitch is:

> Talumi is the tool for signed translations. It allows professional translators to work faster, with AI cross-checks. A human reviews critical points, validates the translation, and signs it.

The proposed communication tagline is: **“Talumi, the translator’s ally. Transform AI capabilities into a professional service that you control, sign, and sell.”** The opposition expressed in discussions between Talumi as an ally of the profession and AI threatening the profession reflects the project’s intention; it does not become a statement that any use of AI would necessarily destroy the profession.

The proposed trial path uses an original text and an existing translation. It shows flagged passages and an example of a report delivered to the client. A public online exercise would allow understanding the benefit without installing software; plugins would then integrate Talumi into daily work. The final choice between online service, local installation, and integrations will depend on confidentiality and usage constraints.

### Reaching translators

The proposed channels are professional associations, translators who demonstrate their real use, and trainers or teachers. A workshop can confront a translation difficulty, Talumi’s flags, and the professional’s decisions. A short, shareable, and reproducible demonstration on the site is a priority dissemination tool.

A plugin is a means of access and not a guarantee of visibility. Early users must find a concrete reason to try, then to recommend the tool. No partner, association, or institution is considered engaged without contact and agreement.

## 21. A Showcase Based on Real Errors

The showcase must make the problem immediately perceptible: a text can be spectacular in its speed of production and yet contain a sentence that reverses an instruction or commitment. The presentation follows four elements: **original, produced translation, possible consequence, actual reporting obtained with Talumi**.

The proposed sources are studies on professional documents, annotated research corpora, and authorized contributions from translators. Each record retains the system and its version when known, the date, context, source, professional analysis, and reproduction conditions. A historical error does not automatically describe the current capabilities of the same provider.

### A Documented Example of Reversal

UCSF reported in 2019 that an English instruction to suspend a kidney medication was translated into Chinese as an instruction to continue taking it. Researchers judged the error potentially fatal. This is a risk assessment, with no deaths reported in this presentation. This case concerns an older version of Google Translate and does not prove a current failure. Source: https://www.ucsf.edu/news/2019/02/413376/google-translates-doctors-orders-spanish-and-chinese-few-significant-errors

### Corpora to Constitute Initial Cases

HalOmi documents hallucinations and omissions in 18 translation directions: https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.42/

Looking for a Needle in a Haystack annotates more than 3,400 sentences with critical errors and hallucinations in an experimental setting without artificially added noise: https://aclanthology.org/2023.eacl-main.75/

These corpora contain system outputs; they do not prove that a commitment was signed or that actual harm occurred. The example of a German–Italian agreement expressing opposing obligations remains a sought-after illustration, not a verified incident to date.

To demonstrate Talumi, the case is submitted to the system without revealing the expected error. The showcase distinguishes what was detected, missed, or insufficiently explained. Outputs and failures are preserved. Publication rights are reviewed before any dissemination.
## 22. Insurability — An Essential Final Layer

Insurance linked to signed translations is envisaged at a later stage. It would provide financial remediation capacity when a covered error causes damage. This development is considered essential in the long-term vision, without being a prerequisite for the beta site.

The intended objective is an offering combining professional qualification, traceable validation of a document, and coverage by a partner insurer. No insurance contract or partnership is established at this stage.

Insurance does not automatically eliminate the translator’s liability. Defense, indemnification, limits, exclusions, and the obligations of the parties will depend on the contract. The working formula is: **“A controlled translation, signed by a qualified professional and covered by insurance,”** commercially usable only when such coverage actually exists.

The design from the outset includes the retention of versions, validations, controls, and incidents useful for future risk assessment. This data can support discussions with an insurer, without guaranteeing acceptance or a level of coverage.
## 23. Multilingual Contracts — Directions for Further Development

The case discussed concerns a Spanish company that must commit to a 300-page document with a foreign partner. A common language facilitates negotiation but does not guarantee identical interpretations. Talumi aims for trust based on documented review, without promising absolute certainty.

### Three Aligned Versions

One considered option pairs each party’s language with a common English version, used as a reference point during exchanges. The versions are compared in all three directions; the English version itself may contain errors. Any change to an amount, condition, or deadline triggers a re-examination of the corresponding passages.

The common reference lies in documented alignment. The decision on the authoritative version belongs to the parties and their advisors. This arrangement is a pathway for certain significant commitments and not a general obligation to translate into three languages.

### Legal Framework of the Assignment

The file must distinguish contract language, authoritative version, applicable law, competent court, and language of proceedings. An English drafting does not imply the application of English law. The choice of a court in a third country or arbitration is a possibility to be examined with the parties’ counsel, without any presumed universal rule.

The Netherlands Commercial Court in Amsterdam is an example of a jurisdiction allowing certain international commercial proceedings in English under its jurisdictional conditions and required agreement; it may apply foreign law. The translator validates linguistic fidelity within the agreed scope. Arbitration of contractual intent or legal questions may require the parties and their lawyers. These developments guide the future vision; they do not delay the product’s experimentation and its adoption by translators.
## 24. E2C: Talumi’s Primary Field

International business documents circulate from English to English (E2E). The parties sign the same English text, which is authoritative. Translation serves only to understand; it is not binding.

Talumi positions itself at the point where English becomes the text of a consumer in their own language (E2C, English to consumer). On a Polish package, an Italian leaflet, or Portuguese terms of use, the local version is the only one the consumer reads. No English text accompanies the product. It is this version that informs, warns, and binds.

European law mandates this local version. For foodstuffs, Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 requires that mandatory information appear in a language easily understood by consumers in the Member State where the product is marketed. Medication leaflets, toy warnings, and cosmetic labels follow the same logic.

The E2C field brings together the conditions where Talumi adds the most value: a short text, a direct consequence for an individual (allergen, dosage, warning), no reference version readable by the end reader, and a single English source rendered into many languages simultaneously.
## 25. The Credit Model and Resale by the Translator

The freelance translator credits their Talumi account, then uploads their document in PDF format. Talumi calculates the price of the review and displays it. The translator sets the price they charge their client.

They receive in return:

- the translated document, documented and highlighted according to risk areas and points to validate;
- the raw text, ready for delivery;
- the Talumi invoice intended for the client, which only mentions the price set by the translator.

The price of Talumi remains between Talumi and the translator. The translator alone decides their margin. Talumi becomes the production tool for a service they sell under their own name.

### Facing a Cheaper Competitor

Since the code is open, a third party can install Talumi and offer a review at a lower price. The model holds for four reasons.

1. **The certificate remains unique to Talumi.** The client verifies the certificate on Talumi’s website, in the register of qualified translators. A competing service can review a text; it cannot issue a verifiable Talumi certificate.
2. **The database of difficult cases remains in the service.** The difficulties validated by translators feed the memory described in section 8. It lives within the hosted service and grows with each assignment. A competitor starts with an empty memory.
3. **The AGPL license brings improvements back.** A competitor who modifies the engine and offers it online must publish their changes. Talumi can incorporate them.
4. **Talumi’s price remains low.** The cost of computing a review is minimal. A low price removes the incentive to build a competing service, with its hosting, reputation, and support.

A competitor who installs Talumi’s code can also become a partner, following the model of Odoo partners.
